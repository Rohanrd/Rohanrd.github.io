Research

My research lies at the intersection of artificial intelligence, computer vision, and healthcare. Across my academic and industrial work, I have been interested in a common question: how can we build AI systems that meaningfully understand complex visual information and ultimately become useful, reliable tools in real-world healthcare?

My research journey began with broader problems in computer vision and image analysis, spanning image restoration, registration, segmentation, image fusion, video analysis, and deep learning. During my doctoral research at the Indian Institute of Technology Delhi, this foundation evolved into a focused investigation of AI for neurosurgical skill assessmentdeveloping datasets, computer vision methods, and learning frameworks for understanding surgical activity and objectively evaluating technical performance were the major focus area.

Today, as a Senior AI Scientist at GE HealthCare, my research has evolved further toward trustworthy and explainable computer vision AI. I work on understanding model behaviour, identifying sources of unreliability, and developing approaches that can make AI systems more dependable and interpretable in healthcare.

My broader research trajectory can be viewed as a progression from understanding images, to understanding surgical activity, to understanding human performance, and now toward building AI systems that can be trusted, adapted, and actively assist clinicians.

Research Journey

Rather than viewing these as separate research topics, I see my work as a progression across increasingly challenging forms of visual understanding and increasingly consequential healthcare applications.

Early Research

Computer Vision & Medical Imaging

My early research established a foundation in computer vision and image analysis, including image restoration, motion deblurring, image registration, image fusion, image segmentation, video summarization, and feature selection. These projects exposed me to the challenges of extracting meaningful information from imperfect visual data and applying learning-based approaches to real-world problems.

This broader computer vision foundation later became the basis for my work on substantially more complex visual problems in healthcare and surgery.

Doctoral Research · IIT Delhi

AI for Surgical Skill Assessment

During my PhD, I focused on the problem of automated and objective assessment of neurosurgical skills. The central idea was to move beyond subjective evaluation by developing AI systems that could understand what happens during surgery and quantify technical performance.

This required solving several interconnected computer vision problems: recognizing surgical actions in video, identifying and segmenting surgical instruments, and evaluating the quality of the final surgical outcome. My work therefore brought together datasets, deep learning, temporal video understanding, object and instance segmentation, and skill assessment.

Doctoral Research · IIT Delhi

Understanding Surgical Activity

One part of this research focused on enabling AI to understand what is happening during a surgical procedure. This included temporal action segmentation of neuroendoscopic videos and instance segmentation of surgical instruments.

The work addressed challenges that are particularly important in medical applications, including limited annotated data, fine-grained visual structures, and temporal variability. This led to methods for video augmentation, curriculum learning, and improved query initialization for transformer-based instance segmentation.

Doctoral Research Translation

From Research to Clinical Translation

The next step was to use these capabilities to answer a more meaningful question: how well is the surgeon actually performing?

This resulted in research on automated assessment of microsuturing, microdrilling, and endoscopic neurosurgical skills, supported by purpose-built datasets and AI frameworks. A major translational outcome of this work was NeuroMentor, an AI-based neurosurgical skills evaluation and training system designed to bring objective assessment closer to practical surgical education.

This experience shaped an important aspect of my research philosophy: AI research is most valuable when advances in algorithms and computer vision can ultimately translate into tools that address meaningful problems faced by clinicians and trainees.

Current Research · GE HealthCare

From “Can AI Understand?” to “Can We Trust AI?”

My current work builds on this experience but shifts the central question. In healthcare, it is not sufficient for an AI system to make accurate predictions; we also need to understand when and why those predictions can be trusted.

As a Senior AI Scientist at GE HealthCare, I therefore focus on reliability, explainability, model behaviour, and failure modes in computer vision AI. A key theme is developing methods that can identify, understand, and mitigate sources of unreliability in AI systems and the data used to train them.

Emerging Direction · GE HealthCare

Toward Promptable & Assistive Medical AI

My recent work also explores a broader direction: moving from AI systems that simply predict toward systems that can be configured, evaluated, and used interactively by clinicians.

Two recent patent filings reflect this direction. One explores prompt-driven creation of specialized medical imaging models, while the other explores AI-guided ultrasound acquisition. Together, they represent an emerging interest in AI systems that can adapt to a clinical requirement and actively assist the user rather than functioning only as passive prediction engines.

Research Through-Line

Across these projects, my research has progressively moved through several levels of AI capability:

  • Understanding visual data through computer vision and medical image analysis.
  • Understanding surgical activity through video understanding and instrument segmentation.
  • Understanding human performance through automated surgical skill assessment.
  • Translating AI research into clinical tools for surgical education and training.
  • Understanding and improving AI reliability through trustworthy and explainable AI research.
  • Moving toward interactive medical AI that can be adapted to clinical requirements and actively assist clinicians.

Ultimately, I am interested in building AI systems that do more than achieve strong benchmark performance—systems that can understand complex clinical visual information, communicate their limitations, adapt to real-world needs, and earn the trust required for healthcare applications.
