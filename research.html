Research

My research interests lie at the intersection of artificial intelligence, computer vision, and healthcare, with a focus on developing reliable and clinically meaningful AI systems. My work spans both fundamental computer vision research and its application to challenging problems in medical imaging and healthcare.

During my doctoral research at the Indian Institute of Technology Delhi, I worked on the automated assessment of microscopic and endoscopic neurosurgical skills. This research involved developing datasets, learning frameworks, and computer vision methods for understanding surgical procedures, identifying surgical actions and instruments, and objectively evaluating technical performance.

I currently work as a Senior AI Scientist at GE HealthCare, where my research focuses on explainable and trustworthy computer vision AI. My present work is aimed at understanding, evaluating, and improving the reliability of AI systems so that their predictions can be better understood and trusted in real-world healthcare applications.

Research Areas

Trustworthy and Explainable AI

My current research focuses on building reliable, explainable, and trustworthy computer vision systems for healthcare. This includes studying model behaviour, reliability, failure modes, and mechanisms that can provide greater confidence in AI-assisted decision making.

Neurosurgical Skills Assessment

My doctoral research investigated AI-based evaluation of microsuturing, microdrilling, and endoscopic neurosurgical skills. The objective was to develop automated and objective approaches for assessing surgical performance, reducing dependence on subjective evaluation by experts.

Surgical Video Understanding

I worked on temporal action segmentation of surgical videos to identify and localize surgical actions over time. A major focus was learning from scarce annotated data, leading to the development of video-specific augmentation and curriculum-learning strategies.

Surgical Instrument Segmentation

Another component of my research focused on surgical instrument instance segmentation and classification. This work investigated the limitations of transformer-based instance segmentation methods for fine-grained surgical instruments and explored improved query initialization and multimodal learning strategies.

Medical Imaging and Computer Vision

My broader research experience includes medical image analysis, image segmentation, image classification, image registration, image restoration, deep learning, and computer vision, with applications spanning neurosurgery, radiology, heritage imaging, surveillance video analysis, and other domains.

Selected Research Contributions

My doctoral work resulted in several datasets and AI frameworks addressing different components of automated surgical skills assessment. Three representative projects are highlighted below.

Micro-suturing Skills Evaluation

In this work, we developed a multi-task learning framework for evaluating neurosurgical micro-suturing skills from the final surgical outcome. The work addressed the challenges posed by small, long-tailed training datasets and variations in magnification and suture size. We also introduced the first dataset specifically targeting the micro-suturing task and demonstrated that the proposed approach could outperform human experts and existing state-of-the-art methods.

VideoCutMix: Temporal Segmentation of Surgical Videos in Scarce Data Scenarios

Presented at MICCAI 2024, VideoCutMix introduces a flow-consistent, video-specific augmentation strategy for temporal action segmentation when annotated surgical data are scarce. The method generates pseudo-action boundaries while preserving temporal consistency and is combined with a sample-hardness-inspired curriculum learning strategy. The work also introduced the Neuro-endoscopic Trainee Simulator (NETS) dataset for non-robotic neuroendoscopic action segmentation.

Learnable Query Initialization for Surgical Instrument Instance Segmentation

Presented at MICCAI 2023, this work investigated why transformer-based instance segmentation approaches struggle with fine-grained surgical instruments in low-data settings. We identified incorrect query initialization as a key limitation and proposed a Query Proposal Network (QPN) and a learnable Query Proposal Decoder (QPD) to improve the initialization of instance segmentation queries.

Current Research

At GE HealthCare, my current research focuses on the reliability and explainability of computer vision models used in healthcare. One of the key directions of this work is understanding how AI systems behave under challenging conditions and developing approaches that can make their predictions more dependable and interpretable.

Relax: Reliability Enhancement by Limiting Adverse Exertions

I am currently working on RELAX (Reliability Enhancement by Limiting Adverse Exertions), a work exploring mechanisms for improving the reliability of AI systems by limiting adverse model behaviour. The manuscript has been submitted to Nature Scientific Reports. Co-authors include Raghunathan Selvam, Prasad Sudhakar Murthy, Rahul Venkataramani, and Chandan Aladahalli.

MIRROR: Multi-model Inference and Regional Reasoning for Recognizing Annotation Errors

MIRROR explores the use of multi-model inference and regional reasoning for identifying annotation errors in medical imaging. The work was accepted as a short paper at the Medical Imaging with Deep Learning (MIDL) conference. It reflects my broader interest in developing AI systems that can reason about their own predictions and identify potential sources of unreliability in medical data and model outputs.
